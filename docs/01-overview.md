# 01 · Overview

Physics Atlas explains hard physics through experiments you can hold. It borrows the structure of a periodic table: an index of tiles that each open a deep, layered page.

## Goals

- **Intuition first.** Every topic opens with a 3D scene and a plain-language story. Math comes later and is optional.
- **Equations that respond.** The headline equation is broken into terms, and each term is linked to the slider or readout that drives it.
- **Honest physics.** Solvers are tested against analytic results. Scenes show their own accuracy (energy drift, norm). Every page ends with what the model leaves out.
- **Static and fast.** No backend. Each topic is a lazily loaded chunk of about 30 kB on top of shared Three.js and KaTeX chunks.

## Audience

The pages are layered for readers from curious high-school students to graduate students. Layer 1 needs no math. Layer 2 uses algebra and some calculus. Layer 3 includes derivations at undergraduate level.

## Scope of v0.1

There are six live topics across classical mechanics, relativity, quantum mechanics and string theory. Fifteen roadmap tiles show where the atlas grows next.
